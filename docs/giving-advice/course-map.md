# Where this sits in your Level 3

Everyone giving advice with the portal should hold the City & Guilds **Level 3 Award in Energy Awareness (6281-01)**. The portal shows you what is happening in a home. The course is what lets you explain it and advise on it.

This page maps each part of the portal to the part of your **Candidate Assessment Reference Manual** that covers it. Page numbers are the printed ones in version 1.4 (May 2025). If your copy is a different version the pages may have moved, but the element and heading will still find it.

The manual belongs to National Energy Action and is not reproduced here. Use your own copy. If you do not have one, ask your programme coordinator.

## Where the portal and the manual differ

- **Humidity.** The portal flags days at or above 65% relative humidity. The manual quotes the Building Research Establishment: mould spreads where humidity stays at 70% for long periods. The portal is warning you early. Use the manual's figure when you explain it to a resident.
- **Modelled versus standard.** An EPC and SAP assume a standard household. The portal works from this household's own meter. If the two disagree, the household's real use is the better guide to their bills, and the EPC is the better guide to the building.
- **Prices and schemes date quickly.** The manual's prices and payback figures are from its publication date, and schemes open and close. The portal's rates are current. For schemes, check before you signpost.
