**Tab: Characteristics**

The better the portal knows the building, the better its heating and cost figures are. Ten minutes on a first visit makes every later figure more useful.

The tab is laid out in this order:

1. **What this home pays**
2. **What we already hold**
3. **Planning and the area**, where the council publishes it
4. **Energy bill**
5. **Property characteristics**, the form, which starts with **Current tariff**

## What this home pays

The electricity and gas unit rates and daily standing charges the portal is using, with the tariff name where it is known. The top of the card says where the rates came from:

| Shown as | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **read from the meter** | The rates were read from the smart meter |
| **from the supplier** | The rates came from the supplier |
| **Ofgem price cap** | The tariff is not known, so the regional price cap is used in its place. Costs for these homes are estimates |
| **entered by hand** | Someone typed the rates in |
| **from a bill** | The rates were read from an uploaded bill and checked by an adviser |

Under each fuel a label says what kind of tariff it is:

| Shown as | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **on the price cap** | The price changes every quarter without notice | Worth raising when the cap is about to change |
| **on a deal** | Cheaper than the cap and protected until the deal ends | Check when it ends. See **Current tariff** below |
| **above the cap** | Paying more than the price cap | Worth a conversation about the options |
| **time-of-use** | The price depends on the time of day, so it cannot be compared with the cap | Look at when the home uses its energy |

A household can be on a different kind of tariff for each fuel. A short sentence under the label explains the position in plain words.

## What we already hold

Details gathered automatically, such as the EPC rating, the type of home, the boiler and its efficiency, and the building's footprint. If the EPC is old, remember the home may have been improved since.

## Planning and the area

Shown where public records cover the address.

| Row | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Conservation area** | Whether the home is in one, and its name |
| **Listed building** | Whether it is listed, and the grade. "List entry within 10 m — confirm on visit" means the record may belong to a neighbouring building |
| **Article 4 direction** | Whether the council has removed some usual permitted changes in this area |
| Deprivation | How deprived the neighbourhood is, from 1 (the most deprived 10% in England) to 10 (the least) |

**"Not published by the council"** means the council does not publish that information, so the answer is unknown. It does not mean no.

Where any of the first three is yes, the card reminds you to check with the council before recommending external wall insulation, visible solar panels or an air-source heat pump. A listed building needs consent for most changes, inside and out.

The deprivation figures describe the neighbourhood, about 1,500 people, not this household. Never use them to make assumptions about the resident.

## Energy bill

Upload a resident's bill so the portal knows their real tariff.

1. Select **Upload bill** and choose the file. It must be a PDF, JPG or PNG, up to 10 MB.
2. The bill is read by the LDS-hosted model. The tariff name, type, rates, fixed deal end date and exit fee appear for you to check.
3. Check every figure against the bill. Anything marked **check** is something the model was unsure of.
4. Select **Save to characteristics**, or **Discard** if it is wrong. Nothing is saved until you select **Save to characteristics**.

If the bill could not be read, enter the tariff by hand in **Current tariff**. If a bill shows only one fuel, the other fuel's rates may be on a separate bill.

The card lists the bills already on file, with the date each was uploaded and whether it was saved. Bills hold personal details, so meter numbers on them are shown partly hidden and are never copied onto the household's record.

## Property characteristics: the capture form

### Current tariff

The first part of the form. The meter cannot tell the portal when a fixed deal ends, so record it here from the bill.

- **"Tariff now:"** shows the tariff names already known, and where they came from.
- **Tariff not confirmed** means the meter's rates are not the price cap and nothing names the tariff. Select **Upload a bill**, or type the details in.
- Fill in **Supplier**, **Tariff name (as on the bill)**, **Type**, **Fixed deal end date**, **Exit fee per fuel (£)** and **Payment method**.

When a fixed deal end date is recorded, the portal warns four weeks before the deal ends. When a fix ends, the home moves onto the price cap unless the resident chooses a new deal.

A tariff name you add is picked up by the portfolio overnight.

### The rest of the form

Fill in what you can see or what the resident tells you. Leave anything you do not know at the dash. **What you enter wins** over the automatic details.

The form is grouped as follows:

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Dwelling basics | Type of home, bedrooms, floor area, age band, tenure |
| Walls, roof and loft, floor | Construction, insulation, loft depth, loft hatch |
| Windows and draughts | Glazing, frames, draught-proofing |
| Space heating | Main fuel, system, approximate age, radiators or other |
| Heating controls | Timer, room thermostat, radiator valves, usual thermostat setting, hours heated a day |
| Secondary heating | Type and how often it is used |
| Hot water | Source, cylinder, cylinder insulation |
| Ventilation, damp and condition | Ventilation type, damp or mould |
| Lighting and appliances | Low-energy lighting, high-load appliances, solar panels, battery |
| Adviser assessment | Your overall note on condition and advice given |

Select **Save capture** when you have finished. The file then shows when it was last captured and by whom.

This is observed advice, not a lodged EPC.

## Welfare observations

The last part of the form covers underheating or rationing, likely eligibility for the Priority Services Register, and other signs of vulnerability.

!!! warning "Consent first"
    Record welfare observations **only** after the resident has agreed, and tick **Consent confirmed to record welfare observations**. These entries are restricted, are never shown to the resident, and are never exported.

Things spotted on a visit that the handyperson could fix, such as draughts or an uninsulated loft hatch, can be referred from the [Handyperson](../households-and-visits/handyperson-referral.md) tab.
